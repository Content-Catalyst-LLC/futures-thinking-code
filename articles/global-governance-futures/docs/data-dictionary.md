# Data Dictionary

## governance_profiles.csv

Synthetic global governance future profiles.

- `institutional_capacity`: Authority, staffing, mandate clarity, operational capacity, and coordination ability.
- `legitimacy`: Perceived fairness, trust, consent, and public credibility.
- `legal_authority`: Strength of law, compliance mechanisms, treaty systems, and dispute resolution.
- `finance_capacity`: Ability to finance commitments, adaptation, public goods, and implementation.
- `collective_action`: Ability to overcome free-riding and coordinate shared responsibility.
- `technology_governance`: Capacity to govern AI, cyber, platforms, data, digital infrastructure, and standards.
- `planetary_risk_coordination`: Capacity to address climate, biodiversity, oceans, food, health, and systemic ecological risk.
- `adaptive_learning`: Ability to monitor weak signals, update assumptions, revise rules, and learn from failure.
- `public_accountability`: Transparency, civic oversight, public review, and enforceable accountability.
- `representation_equity`: Inclusion of vulnerable states, communities, workers, Indigenous peoples, civil society, and future-generation interests.

## governance_scenarios.csv

Alternative futures used to stress-test global governance capacity.

## governance_strategy_options.csv

Strategy options for multilateral reform, bloc-risk reduction, regional resilience, technocratic accountability, platform accountability, planetary risk finance, justice-centered governance, and emergency stabilization.

## governance_risk_indicators.csv

Early-warning and systemic global-governance risk indicators.

## institutional_records.csv

Institutional capacity records across diplomacy, monitoring, finance, law, science, implementation, public accountability, and civic space.

## adaptive_governance_pathways.csv

Simulation parameters for governance capacity, legitimacy, system stress, and adaptive learning over time.
